# Care, vocabulary, and governance stance

This note records how "The Archive Argues With Itself" handles Indigenous content and sensitive
historical material, what vocabulary the tool uses in its own voice and why, and, plainly, what the
tool does and does not claim about governance. It is grounded in the CARE Principles for Indigenous
Data Governance and the Local Contexts Notices. It is the tool's own account; it is not a community
agreement and not an institutional policy.

## The one hard line: the record is never changed

The tool never alters, rewords, redacts, truncates, or deletes a scanned page, its OCR text, a stored
passage, a real document or catalogue title, or any displayed historical excerpt. The record keeps its
own words, including offensive or colonial ones. Everything the tool adds is clearly the tool's own
voice, placed alongside the record, never edited into it. All of the changes described here are in the
web frontend and in this docs folder; none of the record-bearing data or the serving endpoints
(/ask evidence, /stories, /pages, /examples) were touched.

## What the tool claims, and does not claim

- The tool is CARE-aligned and Notices-ready. It is NOT community-governed, NOT CARE-certified, and
  NOT endorsed, reviewed, or consented to by any community.
- Real governance Notices (Local Contexts) are generated by the holding institution together with the
  communities concerned, through the Local Contexts Hub. What this tool shows is its own honest
  acknowledgement, worded as such, not an official Notice.
- The tool adds context; it does not speak for any community, and it does not assert a single correct
  term where a term is contested or community-specific.

## Final locked wording (festival)

These two strings are locked. They live in web/src/lib/sensitivity.ts (GOVERNANCE_STATEMENT and
ATTRIBUTION_NOTICE) and render verbatim: the governance statement in About and in both scopes' Gaps
care notes; the attribution notice in the care note and the interstitial.

Governance statement (About and Gaps):

  This tool is aligned with the CARE Principles for Indigenous Data Governance, and informed by the
  Local Contexts Notices and the First Nations principles of OCAP. It is not community-governed, not
  certified, and has not been reviewed or endorsed by any community. Real governance notices are
  created by the holding institution together with the communities concerned, through the Local
  Contexts Hub. This tool only adds context alongside the record. It never changes the record, and it
  does not speak for any community.

Attribution-incomplete notice (shown in the care note and the interstitial for residential-school
records always, and for coerced-sterilization records only when the record shows Indigenous context;
see "How the care layer works" below):

  This is a government record about an Indigenous community, created without that community's
  involvement or consent. It is shown here unaltered. Cultural rights and protocols may apply.

## How the care layer works (three tiers)

Harm-adjacent content is flagged on serve by a detector (src/archive_debugger/flags.py) and rendered by
the web frontend (web/src/lib/sensitivity.ts). The treatment is proportionate: crisis lines belong only
on the heaviest topics, and lighter content gets a calm note and nothing more. Detection is additive --
it only adds to the response's `flagged` field and the care copy shown alongside the record; it never
alters the record or the answer. When more than one tier is present, the heaviest wins (A over B over
C), so a result that touches both a crisis topic and a lighter one gets the crisis treatment.

Tier A -- crisis care (verified crisis lines, from the single source of truth,
src/archive_debugger/crisis.py):

- Residential-school health: always the full treatment -- the tap-through interstitial, the
  attribution-incomplete notice, the contextual note, and the Indigenous-specific lines (the National
  Indian Residential School Crisis Line, Hope for Wellness, and 9-8-8). This content is inherently about
  Indigenous communities, so it is never gated.
- Coerced sterilization: the interstitial, the contextual note, and 9-8-8. This covers coerced tubal
  ligation (coerced / forced / involuntary / compulsory / non-consensual tubal ligation, "tubes tied
  without consent") and sterilization "without consent" generally, not only the eugenics-era "Sexual
  Sterilization Act" language; plain voluntary or contraceptive tubal ligation with no coercion stays
  Tier B. The attribution-incomplete notice and the Indigenous-specific lines (Hope for Wellness and the
  MMIWG line) are added ONLY when the result shows Indigenous context in the question, the answer, or the
  shown evidence. Without that context the result still carries the interstitial, the note, and 9-8-8,
  but makes no claim to be about an Indigenous community. Indigenous context is matched word-bounded, in
  English and French, including the historical terms the record itself uses (Indigenous, First Nations,
  Metis, Inuit, Aboriginal, Native, Indian, autochtone, Premieres Nations, treaty, Eskimo, "band
  council", "on reserve", and so on); bare "band" / "reserve" and "native-born" are excluded as too
  ambiguous.
- Tainted blood (the Krever inquiry) and early HIV/AIDS: a contextual note and the 9-8-8 line, but only
  when the content is about the scandal or the epidemic. A routine disease-transmission answer -- for
  example how hepatitis C is transmitted -- that only incidentally pulls in an HIV or blood-system
  mention from a source title is NOT given the crisis line; it is treated as Tier C instead (see below).
  The line is kept whenever the question or answer is itself about HIV/AIDS or names the blood scandal.

Tier B -- a calm historical-content note, NO crisis line. For sensitive, colonial, or outdated content
that does not warrant crisis resources: human and reproductive sterilization that is not the Tier A
coercion case (tubal ligation, vasectomy, contraceptive sterilization, eugenics-era reproductive
content), and sexual content, especially anything involving children (sexual attitudes, sexual
counselling, child-sexual material). Routine "sexually transmitted" / "sexual health" public-health
content is deliberately not flagged.

Tier C -- a "not current medical advice" note, NO crisis line. For dated health guidance a visitor could
mistake for current advice: immunization and vaccination schedules and doses, and hepatitis
disease-transmission guidance. Administrative or institutional health content (for example a
mental-health board's responsibilities) is not flagged.

How detection reads the content:

- It reads the user's question, the model's answer, and the shown evidence (the passages sent to the
  model or cited), so harm content the visitor asked about or the model composed is caught, not only
  what a served snippet happens to contain.
- It matches French accent-insensitively: the text being scanned is normalized (diacritics stripped) for
  matching only, so accented forms such as "sterilisation forcee", "sang contamine", "Metis", or
  "Premieres Nations" match the ASCII patterns. The displayed record is never altered -- normalization
  applies only to the copy being searched.
- It shows nothing on a refusal: when the tool abstains, with no answer and no record foregrounded, no
  contextual note and no crisis line appear, so a "the record is silent here" result never puts
  harm-adjacent content or a crisis line on screen. The content stays fully searchable and reachable.
- The reparative term crosswalk (below) remains OFF; no displayed excerpt is annotated.

## CARE Principles (how the tool aligns)

- Collective Benefit: the tool surfaces a public record for public understanding, links every claim to
  a source page, and is open source so others can inspect and reuse the method.
- Authority to Control: the tool defers to the communities a record concerns. It keeps sensitive
  Indigenous-identifying content out of the featured/attract surfaces, and it is built so that a term
  list or a Notice can be set by the institution and community rather than asserted by the tool.
- Responsibility: the tool states its limits (coverage gaps, the undated share, the abstention rule,
  the non-independent judge) and never frames itself as reaching the present or as neutral.
- Ethics: harm-adjacent material carries a proportionate treatment in three tiers (see "How the care
  layer works"): crisis care with verified crisis lines and, on the heaviest topics, a tap-through
  before the result; a calm historical-content note for sensitive material that does not warrant
  crisis resources; and a not-current-medical-advice note for dated health guidance. The
  attribution-incomplete acknowledgement is shown on only two detected topics (residential-school
  content, and coerced-sterilization content that shows Indigenous context), not on every state
  record about an Indigenous community (see "The attribution-incomplete notice" and its known gap).

## Local Contexts Notices (how the tool is Notices-ready)

The tool draws on the spirit of three institution-facing Notices, as the tool's own acknowledgement:

- Open to Collaborate: the tool is open source and invites the institution and communities to set the
  care treatment, the term crosswalk, and any formal Notices.
- Attribution Incomplete: on the two topics the detector identifies as such (residential-school
  content, and coerced-sterilization content that shows Indigenous context), the tool shows a short
  note naming plainly that this is a state record about an Indigenous community created without its
  involvement (see "The attribution-incomplete notice"). It does not cover every such record; see the
  known gap there.
- Collections Care: sensitive material stays searchable and grounded but is kept out of the featured
  stories, with a proportionate contextual note wherever it is surfaced (and verified support lines on
  the heaviest topics; see "How the care layer works").

These are the tool's acknowledgements in the spirit of the Notices; they are not official Local
Contexts Notices, which only the institution and communities can create.

## The attribution-incomplete notice

The tool shows this notice on only the two topics its detector marks as a state record about an
Indigenous community (ATTRIBUTION_TOPICS in web/src/lib/sensitivity.ts, over the topics detected in
src/archive_debugger/flags.py). Because the notice makes a factual claim about the record ("a
government record about an Indigenous community"), it is shown only where that is established:

- Residential-school health content: always. This content is inherently about Indigenous communities.
- Coerced-sterilization content: only when the result shows Indigenous context (see "How the care layer
  works" for how that is detected). A coerced-sterilization result with no Indigenous context -- for
  example a provincial Sexual Sterilization Act and its institution patients -- still carries the
  interstitial, the contextual note, and the 9-8-8 line, but not this notice and not the
  Indigenous-specific support lines, because it would be inaccurate to call it a record about an
  Indigenous community.

Known gap: a record that is about an Indigenous community but falls outside those two topics carries no
notice. The clearest example is in the featured Alberta story itself: the 1974 page is a provincial
inventory of government services to First Nations and Metis people, a state record about Indigenous
communities, yet it is not residential-school or coerced-sterilization content, so no attribution
notice appears on it. The tool does not try to detect every such record; which records should carry a
notice, and in what words, is for the holding institution to decide together with the communities
concerned.

It is additive, calm, and non-performative, sits alongside the existing crisis lines without crowding
them, and is clearly the tool's own words. The copy is in web/src/lib/sensitivity.ts
(ATTRIBUTION_NOTICE) and renders in both the contextual note and the interstitial.

## Vocabulary decisions

The tool's chrome (labels, headings, care copy, about/methods text) was audited. The record's own
words are never changed. Decisions:

Changed (the tool's own voice):

- About, "Care and consent": removed the claim that the tool "follows the CARE principles ... and the
  First Nations principles of OCAP." Reworded to "aligned with the CARE Principles ... and informed by
  the Local Contexts Notices and the First Nations principles of OCAP," with an explicit, honest
  statement that the tool is not community-governed, not certified, and not endorsed. Reason: "follow
  OCAP" overclaims. OCAP (ownership, control, access, and possession) governs First Nations' control of
  their OWN information; a third-party index of state-created records cannot "follow" it, and claiming
  to is both inaccurate and a governance overclaim.
- Gaps, "A note on care" (both scopes now): same softening from "guided by CARE and OCAP principles" to
  "informed by the CARE Principles and the Local Contexts Notices," plus the attribution
  acknowledgement and the plain "not community-governed, no community has endorsed it." A care note was
  also added to the pilot scope's Gaps page, which previously had none, so both corpora carry the
  stance (the pilot holds residential-school and sterilization content too).
- "harm to Indigenous people" -> "harm to Indigenous communities" in the care notes, which reads as the
  more current and collective phrasing.

Kept deliberately (not changed), and why:

- "National Indian Residential School Crisis Line" (crisis.py): kept. This is the official name of the
  real support line, with a verified number; renaming an official service would misname it.
- "Metis" without the accent (crisis.py): kept. The codebase is ASCII-only by project rule; "Metis" is
  the standard ASCII rendering.
- The detector's regular expressions (flags.py), e.g. "indian residential": kept. These match the
  record's own historical words so the care layer can find the content; they are never displayed to a
  visitor, and changing them would reduce recall and regress the care layer.
- The contextual note copy (sensitivity.ts contextualNoteDefault): kept. It is deliberately
  group-agnostic ("This is a government document from <year> ...") so it never names a specific
  community, which is correct for a note that can fire on broad eugenics-era content.
- All seed and probe question text (eval): not touched. The evaluation gold is frozen (standing rule
  N4) and is not the tool's own voice.

Flagged for Jake's / IAC's decision (not decided here):

- The exact wording of the governance statements in About and Gaps, and of the attribution notice.
  These should be the project's and IAC's call, not the assistant's.
- Whether to enable the reparative crosswalk (below) and the exact term list.

## The reparative term crosswalk (built, OFF by default)

When a displayed excerpt's own wording contains an unambiguous historical/colonial term, the tool can
show a small annotation naming it as the record's historical language and the contemporary term. The
excerpt is NEVER rewritten; the annotation is the tool's, layered beside the unchanged word.

This is OFF by default (CROSSWALK_ENABLED = false in web/src/lib/sensitivity.ts), because even
"unambiguous" cases carry false-annotation risk and the term list should be the institution's and
community's call. The data path and the rendering are built and unit-tested; turning it on is one flag.

The seed list (COLONIAL_TERM_CROSSWALK) contains only clearly unambiguous historical-to-contemporary
pairs:

- Eskimo -> Inuit
- half-breed -> Metis

Deliberately excluded: "Indian" (a legal status under the Indian Act, and part of place and institution
names, so not a single correct replacement), and "Aboriginal" (still used in some official contexts;
"Indigenous" is now generally preferred but the term is not a clear error). The tool does not assert a
single correct term for a contested word.

To enable after review: set CROSSWALK_ENABLED = true, confirm the term list with IAC and, where
possible, the communities concerned, and extend the wiring from the compare excerpt to the evidence
trail. No benign content is annotated while the flag is off.
